# Project 2: *Spread*

## Fuck All

**A web version of Kenneth Fitzgerald's essay Fuck All, a response to Michael Rock's Fuck Content (2005).**

by Valarie Hue and Charitaa Bagry

### Introduction to the article

In *Fuck Content*, designer Michael Rock argues that form is design's real content: designers speak through how things look, not through what they say. In *Fuck All*, Kenneth Fitzgerald pushes back. He points out that most writing about design is written by working designers, whose opinions tend to protect their own business and status. He argues that Rock's idea isn't new; it repeats old Modernist thinking and quietly protects design as a service for wealthy, elite clients. Fitzgerald defends "graphic authorship", where designers make self-directed work, as just as valid as client work. His conclusion: design isn't an empty exercise in form. It's made by and for people, and it always says something about who made it.

### The idea: design in boxes

Everyone seems determined to put design, or "the right way to design", into a box. Every designer has a principle, and that principle usually matches the way they already work. Fitzgerald's essay points this out again and again.

So the page is built from boxes. The title, the metadata, each section number and heading, the pull quotes and the closing cards all sit inside their own rounded blocks, each one a neat, contained opinion. They're all various vibrant colours and seem different at first glance but the idea being that at the end of the day it's just a surface level treatment or change.

Then there are the moments where the essay breaks free. In the strikethrough blocks, a phrase is crossed out with a red line and answered by a second phrase written over it, for example "Quality" struck through and replaced with "Propriety". These represent the moments when designers are able to break free from their self constructed cages, the points where the argument cuts through a box instead of staying inside it.

### Typography: many voices, many rules

We used several typefaces on purpose. Design is full of competing preachings and recommendations on how to "get it right", and the page should feel like that conversation.
We used a lot of bold and heavy typefaces to represent the authority and confidence with which people seem to stand by their argument.

DM Sans: (The essay's body text)
A plain sans serif that reflects Fitzgerald's position: design shouldn't be decoration for its own sake, or just made to look interesting, which is Rock's position.

Bricolage Grotesque: (Pull quotes, section numbers, metadata) A more decorative typeface. It doesn't represent Rock's words. It brings a little of his style into the essay, since the two writers go back and forth so much.

Permanent Marker: (Section headings, the struck-through replacements, the title, the closing word) A handwritten marker that feels like it cuts through all the boxes design gets put into.

Anton: (The big title text and the crossed-out words)  A tall, heavy display face for seemingly the loudest moments on the page. But that spot actually ends up going to the permanent marker because of the context of the design and the fact that the giant font is struck across.

The permanent Marker carries the main idea. Good design comes from paying attention to form and content, and most of all to the right context. There is no single way of working, and no single ratio, that works every time.
